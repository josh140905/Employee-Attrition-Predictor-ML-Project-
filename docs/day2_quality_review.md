## 2. Issue 1: Model Consistency & SHAP Mathematical Alignment

### 2.1 Deficiency Analysis
In `day2_model_report.md`, the primary scoring model selected was **Balanced Logistic Regression** ([models/best_model_day2.pkl](file:///C:/Users/aryan/OneDrive/Desktop/RetainIQ/models/best_model_day2.pkl)), which achieved the highest cross-validated PR-AUC (0.6097) and ROC-AUC (0.8270). However, Section 6 generated SHAP explanations using `shap.TreeExplainer` applied to a completely different model: **Balanced Random Forest** ([models/tree_model_day2.pkl](file:///C:/Users/aryan/OneDrive/Desktop/RetainIQ/models/tree_model_day2.pkl)).

Presenting explanations derived from Model B (Random Forest) to explain predictions generated by Model A (Logistic Regression) is a severe architectural violation:
1. Feature rankings and marginal attributions in tree ensembles reflect non-linear splits, whereas Logistic Regression operates via a linear hyperplane in standardized feature space.
2. An employee could be scored as high risk by Logistic Regression due to high `TotalWorkingYears` and `OverTime`, while the tree model might attribute the risk to complex leaf-level interaction splits that the linear model never computed.

### 2.2 Mathematical Remediation: Native `LinearExplainer`
We re-engineered [src/explainability.py](file:///C:/Users/aryan/OneDrive/Desktop/RetainIQ/src/explainability.py) to natively explain the primary prediction model using `shap.LinearExplainer` with independent background distribution masking:

$$\text{logit}(P(Y=1|X)) = w^T X_{\text{trans}} + b = \phi_0 + \sum_{j=1}^M \phi_j(X)$$

- **Explanation Space**: Log-odds (logit space).
- **Sigmoid Mapping**: The predicted probability is recovered exactly via the logistic link:
  $$P(Y=1|X) = \sigma\left(\phi_0 + \sum_{j=1}^M \phi_j(X)\right) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-(\phi_0 + \sum \phi_j)}}$$
- **Mathematical Reconstruction Tolerance**: Across 294 test instances, the maximum absolute difference between `clf.decision_function(X)` and $\phi_0 + \sum \phi_j$ was **$4.44 \times 10^{-16}$** (exact machine precision).

## 3. Issue 2: Age Feature Audit & Policy Contradiction Resolution

### 3.1 Forensic Verification of Feature Matrix $X$
We executed automated inspection of the runtime pipeline in `best_model_day2.pkl`:
- `features.py` defines `DEFAULT_DROP_COLS = ["EmployeeNumber", "EmployeeCount", "StandardHours", "Over18", "Gender", "MaritalStatus"]`.
- `Age` is **NOT** present in `DEFAULT_DROP_COLS`.
- Therefore, `Age` **IS actively present in the predictive feature matrix $X$** as `num__Age` (standardized numerical feature).

### 3.2 Root Cause of Documentation Contradiction
In `day2_model_report.md`, Section 2.1 included an erroneous assertion:
> *"Architecture Note: Protected attributes (Gender, MaritalStatus, and Age) are extracted into an isolated metadata container $S_{\text{meta}}$ exclusively for post-hoc algorithmic fairness auditing, never entering the model input space."*

Simultaneously, Section 6.1 listed `Age` at Rank 5 with high SHAP importance. This was a documentation contradiction caused by conflating the ADEA auditing group in $S$ with the exclusion list in `DEFAULT_DROP_COLS`.

### 3.3 Final Architectural Decision & Governance Justification
We resolve this contradiction with the following formal engineering decision:

1. **Retention of `Age` in $X$**:
   - `Age` is retained in $X$ for the primary model.
   - *Technical Justification*: In Logistic Regression, $\beta_{\text{Age}} = -0.2673$ ($p < 0.01$). Age correlates moderately with career tenure (`TotalWorkingYears`, $r = 0.680$) and compensation (`MonthlyIncome`, $r = 0.498$). Excluding chronological age without removing its career proxies merely forces the model to reconstruct age indirectly via collinear variables, destroying interpretability while failing to prevent indirect proxy discrimination. Explicit inclusion allows the model to control for age directly.
2. **ADEA Governance Rules**:
   - Because `Age` is protected under the Age Discrimination in Employment Act (workers $\ge 40$), model outputs must never be used to make adverse compensation, discharge, or promotion decisions.
   - The platform UI must clearly display that age serves as an organizational demographic baseline, and retention interventions must focus on actionable workplace factors (overtime reduction, managerial support, commute accommodation).

### 4.2 Corrections of Prior Assertions

1. **Retraction of "Virtually Perfect Parity"**:
   - At the default operational threshold ($p=0.50$), Female FPR is **26.00%** compared to Male FPR of **18.37%** (Disparity Ratio = **1.41**). Female non-attriting employees are **41% more likely** to receive a false positive flight-risk flag than male colleagues.
   - At $p=0.75$, while FPR happens to match (0.0600 vs 0.0612), Male False Negative Rate surges to **74.19%** (23 of 31 departing men missed) compared to 50.00% for females (Disparity Ratio = 0.67).
2. **Retraction of "ADEA Age Safety"**:
   - At $p=0.50$, younger workers receive 2.5 times more false alarms (FPR 27.16% vs 10.59%), while older workers who actually depart suffer a **56.25% False Negative Rate** (more than double the younger cohort's FNR of 25.81%).
   - At $p=0.75$, **81.25% of departing older workers are missed** (13 of 16 exits undetected).
3. **Mathematical Impossibility of Simultaneous Parity**:
   - In accordance with established algorithmic fairness theory (Kleinberg et al., 2016; Chouldechova, 2017), when base rates differ across groups, **it is mathematically impossible to simultaneously equalize False Positive Rates, False Negative Rates, and Positive Predictive Values** unless the model has perfect 100% accuracy.
4. **Legal Compliance Must Not Be Claimed**:
   - No statistical metric or confusion matrix parity constitutes legal compliance under Title VII, the ADEA, or EEOC guidelines. Legal compliance is a comprehensive legal evaluation of business necessity, job-relatedness, and selection procedures under the Uniform Guidelines on Employee Selection Procedures (UGESP), not a property of a mathematical confusion matrix.

## 5. Issue 4: Threshold Selection & Generalization Audit

### 5.1 OOF Optimization vs. Held-Out Generalization Collapse
In `day2_model_report.md`, threshold $p^*=0.75$ was selected via grid search on Out-of-Fold (OOF) cross-validated probabilities on the training set to maximize F1-score.

The table below contrasts the training OOF expectations against empirical held-out test set performance ($N=294$, 47 attritions):

| Threshold Strategy | Cutoff | Training OOF F1 | Training OOF Recall | Training OOF Precision | Held-Out Test F1 | Held-Out Test Recall | Held-Out Test Precision | Test Accuracy |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Default** | $p = 0.50$ | 0.5153 | 75.26% | 39.18% | **0.4615** | **63.83%** (30 / 47) | 36.14% (30 / 83) | 76.19% |
| **Cost-Sensitive** | $p = 0.61$ | 0.5621 | 63.68% | 50.42% | **0.4634** | **40.43%** (19 / 47) | 54.29% (19 / 35) | 83.33% |
| **F1-Tuned (OOF)** | $p = 0.75$ | **0.6023** | 54.21% | **67.76%** | **0.4103** | **34.04%** (16 / 47) | 51.61% (16 / 31) | 84.35% |

```
Generalization Drop (Training OOF -> Held-Out Test Set):
Threshold p = 0.50 : F1 drops from 0.5153 to 0.4615 (-10.4% relative drop)
Threshold p = 0.75 : F1 drops from 0.6023 to 0.4103 (-31.9% severe relative drop)
```

### 5.2 Technical & Operational Takeaways
1. **Severe Threshold Overfitting at $p=0.75$**:
   - The threshold $p^*=0.75$ overfit the training fold probability distribution. When transferred to the test set, **F1 collapsed from 0.6023 to 0.4103**, and Recall fell from 54.21% to **34.04%**. The model missed nearly two-thirds of departing employees (31 out of 47 departures undetected).
2. **Superiority of the Default Operational Cutoff ($p=0.50$)**:
   - On the unseen test set, the default threshold $p=0.50$ actually **outperforms $p=0.75$ in F1-score (0.4615 vs 0.4103)** while capturing **63.83% of departures** (30 of 47 caught).
3. **Strict Compliance Policy**:
   - We strictly adhere to best practices: **we do not re-tune thresholds against the test set**. Re-optimizing against the test set would violate generalization integrity.
   - For enterprise HR operations, $p=0.50$ is recommended as the primary surveillance threshold to maximize employee retention interventions.

## 6. Issue 5: Model Calibration Audit

### 6.1 Empirical Calibration Evaluation
Section 3.3 of `day2_model_report.md` claimed that *"Regularized Logistic Regression with inverse class weighting learns well-calibrated hyperplane boundaries"* and that the tree model was *"calibrated and serialized"*.

### 6.3 Statistical Explanation & Product Guidance
1. **Mathematical Cause of Miscalibration**:
   - Specifying `class_weight='balanced'` assigns penalty weights $w_1 \approx 3.1$ and $w_0 \approx 0.6$ (ratio 5.2:1).
   - In Logistic Regression, balanced class weighting is mathematically equivalent to adding an artificial intercept shift of $\ln(5.2) \approx +1.65$ to the decision function.
   - This shifts the entire score distribution upward, causing the model to predict an average probability of **34.63%** when the true incidence is only **15.99%**.
2. **Product & UI Recommendation**:
   - Describing these models as "calibrated" was factually incorrect and is hereby retracted.
   - The predicted outputs from `predict_proba()` are **relative risk rankings**, not true frequentist event probabilities.
   - In the HR dashboard UI, predictions must be presented as a **"Flight Risk Index (0–100)"** or categorized into ordinal bands (High / Medium / Low), rather than telling managers that an employee has an exact "50% chance of quitting".
